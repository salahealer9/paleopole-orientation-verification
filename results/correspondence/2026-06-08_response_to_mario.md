Dear Mr. Buildreps,

Thank you for this, and in particular for two things I want to acknowledge first, because they are the most consequential parts of the whole exchange: your confirmation that script 11 implements your peak-finding rule faithfully, and your acceptance that the published "100%" and "99.999%" figures rest on a uniform baseline the data do not support. Revising those figures is the right call, and I'm glad the work was useful to that end.

On your three further points, rather than argue them over email I have put the responses into the record itself, so they carry the same prominence as the conclusion. Version 3.1 reflects all of this; each point is answered where you can inspect the code and the numbers.

Point 1 — that the null models do not preserve the East/West hemispheric asymmetry — was worth testing directly, so I did, under a specification I pre-committed in the analysis log before running anything. Script 12 re-runs the nulls permuting bearings only within hemispheres, preserving that asymmetry by construction; script 13 then asks whether the asymmetry is itself a consequence of the latitude clustering. The result (§3.8.6): preserving the asymmetry leaves no signal at Pole II, and at Pole III only a site-level residual that disappears once spatial replication is also removed — and the asymmetry itself is about 96% reproducible from the latitude distribution plus great-circle geometry alone, with no hemispheric input. Because a structure pointing true north sends its great circle through the pole, reaching a far-northern latitude on the 47°W meridian requires a small deviation whose sign is fixed by which side of that meridian the structure sits on; the East/West asymmetry is the aggregate of that geometric necessity. Preserving it therefore cannot serve as independent evidence for a pole. The Pole III contributors also split 73 West / 46 East, so it is not a single-hemisphere effect to begin with.

Point 2 — the dating evidence — I take your account of the archaeological record at face value: model-independent dates at the precision required largely do not exist for these structures, and orientation-derived dates would be circular against your model. I agree on both. That is exactly why the report does not claim to have resolved cross-site independence; it discloses it as an open assumption and lets the conclusion stand under it, rather than asserting an answer the evidence cannot support. The separate, narrower point is settled by the database itself: the 29 entries dominating Pole III are eight locations with multiple bearings each, which are not independent observations whatever their dates.

Point 3 — the null finding at Pole I — is addressed in the report (Appendix E author's note). Briefly: the test measures clustering above the geometric baseline, and the current pole is where great circles from all meridians converge, so a high count there is expected by geometry. Observed (95) matching expected (~95) means no excess above that baseline — not that the method is blind to it.

Your 8 June letter is reproduced verbatim as Appendix E, alongside your earlier commentary (Appendix A) and 5 June letter (Appendix D), so the full record of your objections sits beside the analysis at every stage.
