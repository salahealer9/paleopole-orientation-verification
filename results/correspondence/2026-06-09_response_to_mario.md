Dear Mr. Buildreps,

Thank you for this letter. The warmth in it is mutual, and I want to answer it in kind before saying the one thing I need to say about scope.

This exchange has been one of the better experiences of my work, and the reason is you. You opened your data to scrutiny you did not control, you engaged every technical point on its merits, and when the analysis went against a claim you had published, you accepted it and said you would correct the record. That is rare anywhere, and rarer still at the edges where contested ideas live. I have tried to treat your hypothesis as worth testing seriously because I believe it was, and because that is the only kind of testing worth anything. You made that easy by meeting it the same way.

I accept your acceptance of the narrow finding, and I'm glad the probability figures will be revised — that revision is, to my mind, the most valuable concrete thing to come out of all of this.

Now, the one thing I owe you in plain terms. The larger framework you set out in this letter — the hemispheric asymmetry as evidence of a shared orientation choice, the ice-sheet centroid, the glaciation correlation, the crustal displacement, the Earth-expansion mechanism, the flood traditions, the submerged landscapes — is not something I can evaluate, and I am not going to try. Two reasons, and I want to be honest about both. First, it is outside the scope of what this work tested: an orientation-clustering analysis speaks only to whether those orientations point at the proposed latitudes more than chance, and it cannot weigh geology, climatology, or comparative mythology either way. Second, and just as important, it is outside my competence. Those are questions for geologists, climatologists, and people who study oral tradition, and my opinion on them would carry no more weight than any other non-specialist's. It would not be respectful to you or to them for me to wave a hand over fields I have not earned the right to judge.

So I will neither endorse nor dismiss the new paradigm. It simply sits outside what I examined and what I'm qualified to examine. You put it exactly right: it is yours to build, not mine to endorse. What I can say is that the part that fell within my scope was tested as rigorously as I knew how, and you engaged it with more integrity than most. That is the part I'll remember too.

I wish you well with Antiquity Reborn, genuinely. If at any point there is a specific, model-independent piece of evidence bearing on the orientation question — dates from excavation, structure-level provenance — my door is open to looking at it, as it has been throughout. Short of that, I think the record we have built together, with your voice preserved in it at every stage, is a fair and complete account of what this analysis could and could not show.

With respect and gratitude, returned in full,

Salah-Eddin Gherbi
